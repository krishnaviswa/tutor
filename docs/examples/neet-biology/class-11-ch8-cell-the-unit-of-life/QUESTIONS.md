## A. NCERT exercises (important)

Source for all items in this section: end-of-chapter **Exercises**, NCERT Biology Class 11, Chapter 8.

### Exercise 1

Which of the following is not correct?

- (a) Robert Brown discovered the cell.
- (b) Schleiden and Schwann formulated the cell theory.
- (c) Virchow explained that cells are formed from pre-existing cells.
- (d) A unicellular organism carries out its life activities within a single cell.

**Answer:** (a)  
**Why:** Anton von Leeuwenhoek first saw and described a live cell. Robert Brown discovered the **nucleus**. Robert Hooke (not in this option set) observed cork cells.  
**NCERT:** 8.1, 8.2

### Exercise 2

New cells generate from

- (a) bacterial fermentation
- (b) regeneration of old cells
- (c) pre-existing cells
- (d) abiotic materials

**Answer:** (c)  
**NCERT:** 8.2 Virchow, *Omnis cellula-e cellula*

### Exercise 3 (high-yield matching)

Match the following:

| Column I | Column II |
|---|---|
| (a) Cristae | (i) Flat membranous sacs in stroma |
| (b) Cisternae | (ii) Infoldings in mitochondria |
| (c) Thylakoids | (iii) Disc-shaped sacs in Golgi apparatus |

**Answer:** (a)–(ii), (b)–(iii), (c)–(i)  
**NCERT:** 8.5.3.2, 8.5.4, 8.5.5

### Exercise 4

Which of the following is correct?

- (a) Cells of all living organisms have a nucleus.
- (b) Both animal and plant cells have a well defined cell wall.
- (c) In prokaryotes, there are no membrane bound organelles.
- (d) Cells are formed de novo from abiotic materials.

**Answer:** (c)  
**Trap:** Mammalian erythrocytes and sieve-tube cells lack a nucleus (8.5.10). Plant cells have a cell wall; animal cells do not (8.5).

### Exercise 5

What is a mesosome in a prokaryotic cell? Mention the functions that it performs.

**Answer:** Specialised infoldings of the plasma membrane (vesicles, tubules, lamellae). Functions: cell-wall formation, DNA replication and distribution to daughter cells, respiration, secretion, increase of membrane surface area and enzymatic content.  
**NCERT:** 8.4.1

### Exercise 6

How do neutral solutes move across the plasma membrane? Can polar molecules also move across it in the same way? If not, then how are these transported?

**Answer:** Neutral solutes may move by **simple diffusion** along the concentration gradient (no energy). Polar molecules cannot pass the nonpolar lipid bilayer that way; they need **carrier proteins** (facilitated transport). Movement against the gradient using ATP is **active transport** (e.g. Na+/K+ pump). Water by diffusion is **osmosis**.  
**NCERT:** 8.5.1

### Exercise 7

Name two cell-organelles that are double membrane bound. Characteristics, functions, labelled diagrams.

**Answer:** **Mitochondria** and **chloroplasts** (plastids). Both: outer + inner membrane, own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes. Mitochondria: cristae, matrix, aerobic respiration / ATP. Chloroplast: stroma, thylakoids / grana, photosynthesis.  
**NCERT:** 8.5.4, 8.5.5; Figures 8.7, 8.8

### Exercise 8

What are the characteristics of prokaryotic cells?

**Answer:** Bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), mycoplasma / PPLO. Generally smaller and multiply faster. No membrane-bound nucleus (nucleoid). No membrane-bound organelles except ribosomes (70S). Cell wall except mycoplasma. May have plasmids, mesosome, inclusions.  
**NCERT:** 8.4

### Exercise 11

What are nuclear pores? State their function.

**Answer:** Minute pores where the two membranes of the nuclear envelope fuse. Passages for **RNA and protein** movement in both directions between nucleus and cytoplasm.  
**NCERT:** 8.5.10

### Exercise 12

Both lysosomes and vacuoles are endomembrane structures, yet they differ in functions. Comment.

**Answer:** Lysosomes: Golgi-packaged vesicles rich in **acid hydrolases**; digest macromolecules. Vacuoles: tonoplast-bound space with sap / excretory material; in plants up to 90% of cell volume; contractile vacuole in Amoeba (osmoregulation).  
**NCERT:** 8.5.3.3, 8.5.3.4

### Exercise 14

What is a centromere? How does its position classify chromosomes? Diagram.

**Answer:** Primary constriction; kinetochores on its sides. Types: **metacentric** (middle, equal arms), **sub-metacentric** (slightly off middle), **acrocentric** (near one end), **telocentric** (terminal). Satellite: non-staining secondary constriction.  
**NCERT:** 8.5.10; Figures 8.12, 8.13

## B. NCERT Exemplar (important)

Source: https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/exemplarproblem/classXI/biology/keep408.pdf

1. Briefly describe the cell theory.  
   **Answer:** Schleiden (1838) plants; Schwann (1839) animals + plasma membrane / cell wall of plants; together cell theory; Virchow (1855) cells from pre-existing cells. Today: (i) all living organisms are composed of cells and products of cells (ii) all cells arise from pre-existing cells.  
   **NCERT:** 8.2

2. Differentiate RER and SER.  
   **Answer:** RER: ribosomes, protein synthesis and secretion, continuous with outer nuclear membrane. SER: no ribosomes, major site of **lipid** synthesis; steroidal hormones in animal cells.  
   **NCERT:** 8.5.3.1

3. What are plasmids? Role in bacteria?  
   **Answer:** Small circular DNA outside genomic DNA. Confer unique phenotypes, e.g. **antibiotic resistance**. Used to monitor bacterial transformation.  
   **NCERT:** 8.4

4. What are histones? Functions?  
   **Answer:** Basic proteins in chromatin, with DNA, non-histone proteins and RNA.  
   **NCERT:** 8.5.10

5. Group organelles: no membrane / single membrane / double membrane.  
   **Answer:** None: ribosomes, centriole, cytoskeleton (and nucleolus is not membrane-bound). Single: lysosome, vacuole (tonoplast), ER, Golgi, microbodies. Double: nucleus, mitochondrion, plastid.  
   **NCERT:** 8.3, 8.5

### PYQ 10 — NEET 2025

From the statements given below choose the correct option:

- A. The eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S and prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S.
- B. Each ribosome has two sub-units.
- C. The two sub-units of 80S ribosome are 60S and 40S while that of 70S are 50S and 30S.
- D. The two sub-units of 80S ribosome are 60S and 20S and that of 70S are 50S and 20S.
- E. The two sub-units of 80S are 60S and 30S and that of 70S are 50S and 30S.

Options: (1) A, B, C are true (2) A, B, D are true (3) A, B, E are true (4) B, D, E are true

**Answer:** (1)  
**NCERT:** 8.5.6  
**Note:** Svedberg units do **not** add as ordinary numbers (70 ≠ 50+30 as arithmetic of size).

### PYQ 11 — NEET 2025

A specialised membranous structure in a prokaryotic cell which helps in cell wall formation, DNA replication and respiration is

- (1) Mesosome
- (2) Chromatophores
- (3) Cristae
- (4) Endoplasmic reticulum

**Answer:** (1)  
**Trap:** Chromatophores contain pigments in cyanobacteria; they are not mesosomes.  
**NCERT:** 8.4.1
